About

I am a prospective PhD student for Fall 2027, seeking to pursue doctoral research in neural engineering and brain–computer interfaces. I am currently completing my MSc in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), where I also received my BSc in 2023 with a major in Communication and Signal Processing. I also work as a Lecturer in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at East Delta University, Chattogram, where I have been teaching since May 2024.

My MSc research addresses a central obstacle to practical speech BCIs: because EEG varies substantially from one person to another, a decoder trained on one group of people rarely works well for a new user. I study cross-domain generalization of inner speech decoding from EEG signals, with a focus on generalizing across subjects, combining deep learning architectures (CNNs, Transformers, hybrid networks and graph neural networks) with domain adaptation techniques to learn neural representations that remain stable across individuals. The goal is a decoder that can be deployed to a new user with few-trial calibration, rather than lengthy subject-specific training.

I enjoy work that brings together hardware, signal processing and AI. Before turning to BCIs, I worked on robotics, including cooperative multi-robot systems, with publications at ICCIT and ICECE 2024. In my PhD, I want to build neurotechnology that restores lost function for people with paralysis, and to develop neurostimulation approaches for treating psychiatric and neurological disorders.

Research

Research Experience

  1. Cross-Domain Generalization of Deep Neural Decoders for Inner Speech from EEG Signals

    MSc Thesis, BUET (ongoing)

    Supervisor: Dr. Shaikh Anowarul Fattah, Professor, Department of EEE, BUET

    Focus: cross-domain generalization of inner speech decoding from EEG, with an emphasis on generalizing across subjects, using domain adaptation in deep learning architectures so that decoders adapt to new users with few-trial calibration.

    • Applying domain adaptation techniques so that decoders trained on existing subjects transfer to unseen subjects with only few-trial calibration.
    • Processing multi-channel EEG signals and investigating time–frequency, spatial and connectivity-based feature extraction methods for imagined word decoding.
    • Implementing deep learning architectures, including CNNs, Transformers, hybrid networks and graph neural networks, to model spatial–temporal neural dynamics from EEG.
    • Evaluating handcrafted and learned representations with an emphasis on robustness, generalization and neurophysiological interpretability for real-world BCI applications.
